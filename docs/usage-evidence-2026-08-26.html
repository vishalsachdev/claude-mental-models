A conservative reading

Raw keyword counts were not trusted. The May 21 archive spike contained thousands of changelog items masquerading as user prompts, so the report isolates likely direct requests first.

8,106 prompts across 680 recorded sessions form the analytic set. Eligible prompts belong to interactive sessions, are no longer than 2,000 characters, and do not begin with known generated/source prefixes. FTS searches supplied candidates; examples were manually checked.

Monthly bars are normalized within each card. Counts—not bar heights across cards—should be compared between models.

The gaps matter

Absence and uncertainty are part of the result, not footnotes to be hidden.

Two tacit surfaces

Terminal display and input may be used without being discussed. Narrow signatures reduce false positives but cannot observe silent habits.

A late archive floor

All models predate the personal corpus. The 283–357 day gaps are observed lags, not true adoption delays.

May is not behavior

Only four interactive sessions survive the May filter. The raw spike came from imported changelog and headless analysis traffic.

Language overlaps

Slash commands, memory, and configuration cross conceptual borders. Model counts are independent and should not be summed.
